Read This First

Deliveries only: retail cars delivered to the customer with the invoice posted, grouped by invoice posted date. Orders not yet delivered are not included. Months: May to Oct 2026. Weeks: the last 8, W37 to W44 (7 Sep to Sunday 1 Nov 2026). Averages per car. Channels: B2B2C, BCB (supplier Z001), PX and Other (the rest of C2B2C Other). Buy backs, consignment and credited invoices are left out; All is these four channels together.

Everything on this page is deliveries (invoice posted), not orders. A car counts in the week and month its invoice was posted.

These figures join four separate systems that do not share a common key: Apex sales, the buyers' Cars Bought spreadsheet, AutoView costs and the finance records. Lining them up took a lot of manual mapping and checking, car by car and supplier by supplier. Treat the results as a careful best estimate, not an exact reconciliation.

  • Buyer RRPs and estimated prep costs come from the Cars Bought spreadsheet, which is typed in by hand. They are matched to sales on VRM and supplier. Suppliers on the sheet are free text (around 200 different spellings had to be mapped to Apex supplier codes by hand), so cars with a wrong supplier or a mistyped VRM do not match and are missing from the buyer figures.
  • Actual recon and other costs come from AutoView, matched on stock ID. Not every car matches, because Apex sometimes holds more than one record for the same car with different stock IDs. Where AutoView has no match, the Apex vehicle costs are used instead.
  • 0% and rate/cash switches: where a car moved between 0% and rate/cash, a different buyer RRP applies, and the original price may have been set for the other finance type from the one it sold on. The buyer RRP used always matches the finance type the car sold on, and switched cars have been ignored where possible to avoid large false gaps between the three prices, but some may still be in.
  • Original price is the first price the car was advertised at in Apex. Price reductions while in stock fall in the original to selling gap.
  • All prices are full prices with no discounts taken off, so the three can be compared fairly. The selling price is the advertised price the car sold at, before any deal discount. Actual chassis, metal and GPU-M are the real margins, so they do include discounts given (on average only around £15 to £25 a car).
  • Buyer est metal and GPU-M use the buyer's prep estimate plus the actual other AutoView costs (second delivery and buying fees). Where the buyer gave no prep estimate (0 to 35 cars a month) the actual costs are used.
  • GPU-M costs are the standard monthly costs from the management accounts. The same figure is applied to buyer, original and actual, so they do not explain any of the differences.
  • Each chart only counts cars that have every figure it compares, so car counts differ between sections and the averages are not over exactly the same cars.
  • Left out: consignment cars (DPARVAL, DPSANTAN, DPLEX), buy backs and credited invoices. All prices include the standard £199 admin fee, even where it was waived on the deal. 0% deals with a 0% buyer RRP are few (26 to 52 a month), so their figures move around a lot.

Summary

Monthly averages per delivery, all channels and sales types, May to Oct 2026.

  • Prices: buyer RRPs are in line with the original advertised price; the loss comes after. The original price is £0 to £180 above the buyer RRP (closest Aug and Sep, £2 to £6). The full selling price (before any deal discount) then comes in £205 to £355 below the original price, from price reductions while the car is in stock. Net, cars sell at a full price £60 to £250 below the buyer's RRP. Deal discounts on top are small, around £15 to £25 a car.
  • Stock age drives the price reductions. Average days in stock at order ran 36 to 39 days and days to sell 17 to 21 days. September had the most aged stock (18.8% of deliveries over 60 days in stock, against 12% to 15% in other months). Matthew's WK43 GPU-M email found the same link week to week: more stock over 60 days and bigger reductions from the original price pulled chassis margin down.
  • Recon: buyers under-estimate prep every month, by £140 to £210 a car (estimate around £345 to £385 against actual around £525 to £565, so roughly a third too low). PX is the worst, with estimates around £300 against actual around £650.
  • GPU-M: actual comes in £300 to £420 a car below the buyer's estimate every month (buyer est around £1,120 to £1,230 against actual around £760 to £965). About half of the shortfall is the lower selling price and about half is the prep under-estimate. GPU-M costs are the same in all three, so they play no part in the gap.
